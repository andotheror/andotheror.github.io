Sharp Mean Data Selection with Two Points

Pahan Dewasurendra, Johns Hopkins University

Abstract

Suppose a learner must approximate the full-data mean by the unweighted average of two selected examples, with repetition allowed. The excess squared loss is exactly the squared error of the selected mean, so the worst relative loss is a normalized geometric rounding constant. A recent open problem determined this constant in one dimension and, by a computational planar argument, when the dimension and selection budget both equal two. We determine it for every dimension. If $F(d,2)$ is the worst ratio between selected and optimal loss in $\mathbb R^d$, then $F(d,2)=1+\max\left\{\frac13,\frac{d-1}{2d}\right\}.$ Thus the one-dimensional outlier construction remains extremal through dimension three. At dimension four, the extremizer changes to a regular simplex. The upper bound follows from a short positive-matrix argument after a variance-nonincreasing Carathéodory reduction. We also give an exact semidefinite characterization for every dimension and budget. Finally, an explicit asymmetric simplex proves $F(4,3)>6/5$, exceeding both the one-dimensional and regular-simplex lower bounds. This shows why the general phase diagram is more intricate than the solved two-point case.

Keywords: data selection; coresets; mean estimation; squared loss; approximate Caratheodory; subset selection
